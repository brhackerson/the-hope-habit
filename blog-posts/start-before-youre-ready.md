# Start Before You're Ready
## Six things I'd tell you about building a hope practice, if you asked.

*Published: hopeisnotafeeling.substack.com/p/start-before-youre-ready — July 26, 2026*

I have been asked about how I actually built my hope practice, not the story, but the actual mechanics. If you asked me that today, here's what I'd tell you. Not the whole thing. Just the parts that matter most once you're standing where I started.

## Hope Is Something You Do

Don't wait to feel hopeful before you start. The truth is you won't. Hope isn't a feeling you're missing, it's a practice you haven't built yet, and it only exists once you're actually doing it, day after day, whether the feeling shows up first or not.

Don't wait for the pep talk or inspirational speech. It's not coming until intentional action is taken. Motivation doesn't come first and hand you the decision to get in the gym. The action comes first. For me, it was getting to the gym. The motivation, if it shows up at all, shows up after.

## Agency Needs Pathways

Wanting something different won't be your problem. You already do, or you wouldn't be asking. What will actually stop you is not having a plan for what to do when the first path closes, a plateau, an injury, a plan that stops working. Build that plan now, before you need it, who you'll ask, what you'll try next, so a closed door doesn't end the whole thing. Snyder called this pathways thinking, and it works alongside agency, not instead of it.[¹]

If what you're facing doesn't have a fix, pathways thinking still applies, just aimed differently. The route isn't to a cure. It's to the next good hour, the next honest conversation, the next way to show up for someone.

## Respond to the Critic, Don't Fight It

You have an inner critic and it isn't broken. It's doing exactly what it's built to do, pull you back toward comfort and away from risk. Don't argue with it. Don't believe it either. Just get enough distance to see it as a voice instead of a verdict, then ask it a better question than the one it's asking you.

## Receipts Always Beat Plans

Don't trust the plan. Trust the evidence. Once you have a scan, a photo, a number you can point back to, that's worth more than any strategy you haven't tested yet. Start collecting receipts early, even small ones. You'll need them on the days the plan feels like it's failing.

If your numbers aren't moving the direction you want, or can't, the receipt looks different too. It might be a day you got through intact, not a day you improved.

## Celebrations Are Not Optional

When you hit a milestone, stop and mark it. Don't rush straight to the next one. Skip that step often enough and the whole practice runs on the critic's terms, always reacting to what's wrong, never banking what's right.

Not every celebration needs to be the same size. Some will be small and private, a quiet pause you only do for yourself. Others deserve real planning, something bigger you build in advance because the milestone earned it. Skip the small ones and the daily practice runs on the critic's terms. Skip the big ones and you never bank a receipt large enough to draw on when things get hard.

The milestone doesn't have to be physical progress. Getting through a hard day counts too, and it deserves the same pause.

## You Can't Build It Alone

Let others inside your hope practice see the private moments, not just the public ones. You won't build this by yourself, even if it feels like a solitary practice most mornings. The people who see it are the ones who end up handing it back to you exactly when you need it.

## That's It

Not the whole story. Just what I'd want you to know walking in.

Start before you are ready. Do it again tomorrow. And the day after.
